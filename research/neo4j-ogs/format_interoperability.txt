
  Interoperability
The format covers the parts of RDFS and SHACL that describe a domain, not all of either. It is not RDF, not OWL: no
triples, no IRIs, no inference. It is descriptive first: a model says what a domain looks like. Only part of what it
says is enforced in the database.
The coverage tables name fields from the Properties,
Relationships, Constraints and indexes and
Extensions pages. Constructs with no native field travel in custom data. Each row
gets one of these verdicts:
covered: maps to native fields.
partially covered: maps, with limits.
lossy: maps, but drops meaning.
carried: no native field. Travels in custom data.
different mechanism: met another way.
excluded: deliberately out.
n/a: not a construct of that vocabulary.
RDFS coverage
 | 
 | Construct
 | Mapping
 | Verdict
 | rdfs:Class
 | A node. Its labels carry the class: identifier names it. implied and
optional add further labels. The label shorthand expands into labels when the model is decoded.
 | covered
 | rdf:Property
 | A relationship when its range is a class, a
property when its range is a datatype. Nothing records which it came from: a
tool importing RDF decides. A property with several domains or ranges becomes one relationship entry per pair of
ends. RDFS joins them conjunctively. The per-pair entries do not carry that: the combination survives as the
identifier and implied labels of one node.
 | covered
 | rdfs:domain / rdfs:range
 | The from and to ends of a relationship. A
property’s range via its type. Types are Neo4j types, not xsd datatypes: a tool
importing RDF maps them.
 | covered
 | rdfs:label
 | aliases on a node,
relationship or property. A property
also has name (its name in the database when different from its id). A node has no name field.
 | covered
 | rdfs:comment
 | description on the model, a node, a relationship or a property: one string each, capped as the
length rules set out.
 | covered
 | rdfs:seeAlso, rdfs:isDefinedBy
 | reference on a node,
relationship or property: one URI
each. Further values ride custom data.
 | partially covered
 | rdfs:subClassOf
 | No hierarchy to traverse: a tool importing RDF flattens it into the implied labels of a
node. implied and optional are plain labels. Nothing infers over them.
 | covered
 | rdfs:subPropertyOf
 | No field on a node, relationship or property. Property hierarchies are excluded.
 | excluded
 | containers (Bag/Seq/Alt), reification
 | No field. The list types, such as
LIST<STRING>, are ordered lists, not RDF containers. Reification has no carrier.
 | excluded
 | cardinality, enums, patterns
 | Not RDFS constructs, but native here: count,
min_count and max_count on a
relationship end. one_of and pattern on
a property.
 | n/a
SHACL coverage
 | 
 | Construct
 | Mapping
 | Verdict
 | sh:datatype
 | The type of a property, a Neo4j type rather than an xsd
datatype. VECTOR types pair with dimension, and ANY opts the property out. Enforceable as a PROPERTY_TYPE
constraint.
 | covered
 | sh:in
 | one_of on a property of a node or relationship: a list of strings, so
non-string literals must be quoted. Mutually exclusive with pattern. SHACL allows both together.
 | partially covered
 | sh:pattern
 | pattern on a property: a single regex string, with flags such
as (?i) folded into it. Mutually exclusive with one_of. Descriptive, not enforced.
 | covered
 | sh:minCount = 1 on property
 | must_exist on a property: shorthand for an
EXISTS constraint, expanded when the model is decoded. Enforceable via the database schema. A minimum above 1 has
no native field.
 | covered
 | sh:minCount/sh:maxCount on relationship
 | count alone, or
min_count with max_count, on a
relationship end. Descriptive: not yet enforced in the database.
 | covered
 | sh:maxCount > 1 on property
 | A list type, such as LIST<STRING>, or
LIST<ANY> for mixed elements. Gives a list, not a bound on length. Element types are always scalars.
 | lossy
 | sh:class
 | The node or label of a relationship end, pointing at a
node whose identifier label is the class. A relationship is its type and two ends:
the same type between other nodes is a separate entry.
 | covered
 | sh:nodeKind
 | The shape of the model itself: literals live under properties, node references only as relationship
ends. IRIs collapse into relationships and labels, literals into property types. Nothing distinguishes an IRI from a
blank node: blank nodes have no carrier.
 | lossy
 | sh:name, sh:description
 | description on a node, relationship or property. The only name fields are a
property’s (its name in the database when different from its id) and the model’s own. No display name for a node or
relationship.
 | lossy
 | sh:message, sh:severity
 | No native field. Rides custom data. The format
has no severity concept.
 | lossy
 | sh:minInclusive/maxExclusive etc. (value ranges)
 | No native field on a property: only pattern and
one_of restrict values. Rides custom data. See Gaps and caveats.
 | carried
 | sh:minLength/maxLength
 | No native field on a property. Rides custom
data. Not the length caps on the rules page: those bound the model’s own name and
description, not property values. See Gaps and caveats.
 | carried
 | sh:languageIn, sh:uniqueLang
 | No native field. Rides custom data.
Property values carry no language tags.
 | carried
 | sh:equals, sh:disjoint, sh:lessThan (property-pair comparisons)
 | No native field. Rides
custom data. See Gaps and caveats.
 | carried
 | sh:hasValue
 | No dedicated field. Rides custom data. Approximate it with
one_of plus must_exist: a single value, strings only, and it forces presence.
 | carried
 | sh:closed, sh:ignoredProperties
 | No native field. Rides custom data.
Neo4j is schema-optional: a closed world is enforcement policy, not ontology content.
 | carried
 | sh:order, sh:group
 | No native field. Rides custom data. The
nearest native field to styling is a node’s display.
 | carried
 | property paths (inverse)
 | No field of its own: the from and to ends of a
relationship entry express them by direction.
 | different mechanism
 | property paths (alternative/sequence)
 | Not constraints in this format. Anything beyond a single hop rides
custom data.
 | carried
 | sh:not/and/or/xone, qualified cardinality
 | No boolean composition: the
constraint types are only EXISTS, UNIQUE, KEY and
PROPERTY_TYPE.
 | excluded
 | SHACL-SPARQL constraints
 | No SPARQL machinery. Raw text at best in custom
data.
 | excluded
 | SHACL-AF rules/functions
 | No field on the model, deliberately: rules and functions imply inference, which the
format does not do.
 | excluded
Enforcement
A model is descriptive first. The libraries validate the model document itself, not
the data in the database. The database enforces only what its schema can carry.
Enforced in the database:
The identifier label of a node and the type of a relationship: they select what the constraints apply to. The
implied and optional labels are descriptive.
must_exist, unique and key on a property, and the
constraints of a node or relationship: the four constraint types,
EXISTS, UNIQUE, KEY and PROPERTY_TYPE.
Descriptive, not enforced:
The from and to ends of a relationship and their count, min_count
and max_count.
one_of and pattern on a property.
name, description, aliases, reference and dimension.
extensions, and the custom data in them.
Gaps and caveats
Gaps, likeliest to matter first:
Value ranges: sh:minInclusive, sh:maxExclusive and the rest. Plausible in regulated industries (ingredient
classes, dosage ranges). No native field on a property. The route is an owner-defined validation object in
custom data.
String lengths: sh:minLength and sh:maxLength. Cheap to add on the same validation object. Nothing on a
property today. Not the length caps on the rules page: those bound the model’s own name and
description, not property values.
Property-pair comparisons: sh:equals, sh:disjoint, sh:lessThan. Rare in practice. The same route through
custom data.
Caveats:
SHACL validates instance data after loading it. This format is descriptive first, enforced via the database schema
where possible. Validation checks the model document, not the data.
SHACL binds shapes to data with targets such as sh:targetClass. Here the binding is identity: a
node entry is the shape for its identifier label. No target machinery.
